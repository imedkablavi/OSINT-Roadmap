# OSINT Report Template

Use this as a starting point, not a rigid form. A short investigation should stay short.

## Case information

**Title:**  
**Researcher:**  
**Date:**  
**Status:** Draft / Final  
**Handling note:** Public-source research only

## 1. Question

State the exact question the research is trying to answer.

```text
Example: Is the public claim that [X] occurred before [date] supported by independent sources?
```

## 2. Scope

Define what was and was not examined.

**Included:**

- Publicly accessible websites and archives
- Public records relevant to the question
- Public social or media sources where appropriate

**Excluded:**

- Private accounts or restricted content
- Deception or impersonation
- Unauthorized access
- Collection unrelated to the research question

## 3. Executive finding

Write the answer in 2–5 sentences.

Include:

- the main conclusion;
- confidence level;
- the strongest reason;
- the most important limitation.

## 7. Method

Briefly explain what was done so another researcher could reproduce the important steps.

Example:

```text
Searched the exact claim and date variants across two search engines, checked archived versions of the official page, compared the earliest public references, and verified the key date against an independent primary record.
```

Avoid filling this section with every click.

## 8. Limitations

Be specific.

Useful limitations look like:

- historical archive coverage was incomplete between two dates;
- a source gave only a date, not an exact time;
- two secondary reports appear to rely on the same original statement;
- available imagery supports the location but not the capture date.

## 9. Confidence scale

### High

The central conclusion is supported by strong, relevant, and preferably independent evidence, with no unresolved contradiction likely to change the result.

### Medium

The conclusion is supported, but one or more important gaps, dependencies, or plausible alternatives remain.

### Low

Evidence is limited, indirect, inconsistent, or dependent on assumptions that have not been verified.

## Final review

Before publishing or sharing the report, check:

- Every important factual statement has a source.
- Facts and analysis are clearly separated.
- Dates and time zones are consistent.
- Screenshots are not used as substitutes for source URLs when a URL exists.
- Confidence is explained, not just labeled.
- Alternative explanations were considered.
- Unnecessary personal data was removed.
- The conclusion does not claim more than the evidence supports.
